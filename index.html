The special sauce

It grades the job before you waste a week on it.

Most tools help you apply faster. Foot Jar decides whether you should apply at all. Every posting is read end to end and scored against what you actually want, then told to you straight.

  • Read in full, never triaged on a title. One role scored 90 on the title and turned out to require semiconductor wet-process chemistry.
  • Match matrix and honest gaps, with the transferable framing for each one.
  • Salary anchor, target and floor before you are ever asked the question.
  • It is allowed to say no. A verdict that never says pass is not a verdict.

The special sauce

One queue. One bar. Always the next right move.

A job search fails in the gaps: the application you meant to finish, the note you meant to send. Every role carries its own eight-stage timeline, and one bar at the top tells you how the whole search is doing.

  • Sorted by fit, odds or status, so the top of the list is the next thing to do.
  • Stages prove themselves. If the document exists, the stage is complete — the timeline cannot drift from reality.
  • Portfolio odds across every open role, not wishful thinking on one.
  • Opens on your phone, because that is where you check it.

Clear the machine, then impress the person.

Most resume scanners are a paywall around a word count. Foot Jar runs the checks itself and refuses to publish a document that has not passed.

  • Parse integrity — single column, standard headings, safe fonts, clean contact block.
  • Real metrics counted separately from decorative ones.
  • Number padding is a defect, not a score. Dressing "all" up as "100%" gets flagged.
  • Keyword coverage measured against that specific posting.

The special sauce

People hire people.
Robots don't.

Everyone tells you it is about who you know. Nobody tells you what to do when you do not know anybody. Foot Jar combs the data and comes back with a name.

  • The actual hiring manager, the recruiter, and the peers worth knowing — tiered, most useful first.
  • Your own network searched first. A warm introduction beats every cold note combined, and it is usually hiding in a mutual connection you forgot about.
  • Every person verified individually. No claim about anyone that has not been confirmed on their own profile.
  • Notes drafted in your voice — and never, ever sent for you.

AI is not replacing the handshake. It is finding the hand.

Standout feature

It writes like you. Not like a robot pretending to be you.

Generated cover letters read like generated cover letters, and hiring managers have learned the smell. Foot Jar starts somewhere else: it interviews you first, then writes from what you actually said.

  • A guided conversation, once. After that, every document carries your voice.
  • Your real stories, not adjectives. The specifics that only you would know.
  • Never invents a number, a tool, or a title. If you did not say it, it does not appear.

Not open yet. Voice sessions are limited while the first one is still running.

When it opens, you talk and Foot Jar listens. Everything it writes for you afterward sounds like the person who showed up to that conversation.

The long game

Your career doesn't end when the job search does.

Every other tool in this category is built to be abandoned. You get the job, you cancel, and three years later you start from a blank page with a resume you can't find and contacts you half remember.

  • It goes quiet, not away. Between searches Foot Jar drops into a dormant mode that costs almost nothing and keeps running in the background.
  • Your resume stays current. Add a win when it happens, not eighteen months later when you are trying to remember the number.
  • Your network stays warm. Every person, every conversation, every introduction, still there and still searchable in four years.
  • One switch turns it back on. When you need it, you start from a complete record instead of a blank page.

The best time to build your career file is when you don't need it.
