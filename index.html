Your voice

Everyone can smell a generated cover letter.
Including the person reading yours.

Generated cover letters read like generated cover letters, and hiring managers have learned the smell. Foot Jar starts somewhere else: it interviews you first, then writes from what you actually said.

  • A guided conversation, once. After that, every document carries your voice.
  • Your real stories, not adjectives. The specifics that only you would know.
  • Never invents a number, a tool, or a title. If you did not say it, it does not appear.

Not open yet. Voice sessions are limited while the first one is still running.

When it opens, you talk and Foot Jar listens. Everything it writes for you afterward sounds like the person who showed up to that conversation.

What a generator writes

“I am a results-driven marketing leader with a proven track record of driving growth and delivering value across the organisation.”

Says nothing. Could be anyone. They have learned the smell.
What you actually said

“I inherited an email program sitting at 5.27% opens. It is at 21.8% now, on lower volume.”

One sentence, one number, and nobody else could have written it.
After the search

Built to be kept, not cancelled.

Every other tool in this category is built to be abandoned. You get the job, you cancel, and three years later you start from a blank page with a resume you can't find and contacts you half remember.

  • It goes quiet, not away. Between searches Foot Jar drops into a dormant mode that costs almost nothing and keeps running in the background.
  • Your resume stays current. Add a win when it happens, not eighteen months later when you are trying to remember the number.
  • Your network stays warm. Every person, every conversation, every introduction, still there and still searchable in four years.
  • One switch turns it back on. When you need it, you start from a complete record instead of a blank page.
